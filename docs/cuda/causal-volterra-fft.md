# Causal Volterra FFT pricing

The causal FFT engine prices paths whose Volterra source depends on the path
already simulated. It currently has path policies for rough Heston and
quadratic rough Heston (QRH). It shares the FFT descriptors and tuning,
linear-convolution primitive, product observations, calendar policies, row
layout, and payoff reduction with the Gaussian Volterra engine. A new causal
model supplies a kernel policy and one-cell path policy; the scheduler and FFT
logic do not depend on the model.

## Discretization and causal blocks

For the normalized fractional kernel
\(K_H(t)=t^{H-1/2}/\Gamma(H+1/2)\), define the cell averages
\(w_\ell=\Delta t^{-1}\int_{(\ell-1)\Delta t}^{\ell\Delta t}K_H(s)\,ds\).
The far part of the Volterra value at date \(n\) is
\(A_n=\sum_{j<n}w_{n-j}u_j\), where \(u_j\) is the drift and stochastic
source generated by the model at date \(j\). The rough Heston policy adds an
independent normal for the singular cell: its projection on the Brownian
increment is already in \(w_1u_{n-1}\). QRH uses the balanced drift/source
rule of its dense reference implementation.

A path kernel advances up to 32 or 64 dates directly. At a block boundary
\(b\), let \(s=\operatorname{lowbit}(b)\). A cuFFTDx convolution of the newly
known \(u_{b-s},\ldots,u_{b-1}\) with the precomputed kernel spectrum for
scale \(s\) adds the useful output slice to future accumulators
\(A_b,\ldots,A_{b+s-1}\). These accumulators are completed by later blocks or
the short direct loop. The kernel spectrum is computed once per row and scale;
each FFT update packs two real paths in one complex transform. Separate CUDA
launches provide the synchronization required before the next causal block.

The engine groups rows in a configurable batch and paths in a reusable chunk.
The source history and future accumulators share one group-of-eight,
step-major buffer. Models, products, and calendars retain the existing
`PricingRow`, `PathProductObservationAdapter`, and reduction conventions.

## API

Include `model/equity/rough/rough_heston/causal_fft_pricing.cuh` or
`model/equity/rough/quadratic_rough_heston/causal_fft_pricing.cuh`. Choose an
existing product and Volterra calendar policy, then:

1. Call `plan_causal_fft_workspace<ProductPolicy, SchedulePolicy>` with the
   number of dates, paths per price, path chunk size, and row batch size.
2. Allocate `workspace_bytes` device bytes, model and product arrays, and
   price and standard-error output arrays.
3. Call the model's `launch_*_causal_fft_cuda` with the same dimensions,
   workspace, seed and output pointers.

Both bindings use the generic causal engine. Link CUDA consumers to the
`ai_factory_causal_fft` interface target when mathDx is available. A new
product can reuse its existing policy and schedule; a new model supplies
`Parameters`, `PreparedModel`, `State`, `KernelPolicy`, `kernel_parameters`,
`prepare_model`, `initial_state`, `advance_cell`, `spot`, and `log_spot`.
The path chunk must be a multiple of the configured Volterra pricing thread
count (256 in the reference profile). At most 8,192 simulation dates are
supported by the current FFT profile.

## Validation and current limit

`test_causal_fft_pricing_cuda` compares both model policies with a direct
\(O(N^2)\) convolution using identical normals, including 95-, 128-, and
130-date grids, European calls, Asian calls, an up-and-out barrier with early path
termination, prices, standard errors, 513 paths (odd final chunk), and seed
replay. A 252-day product on 504 simulation dates checks the
production rough grid at `dt = 1/504`; the other date counts exercise FFT
boundaries and grid refinement. This checks the FFT and scheduler against the implemented time
scheme. It does not establish the weak convergence of that scheme to the
continuous rough Heston law.

On an RTX 4090 Laptop GPU (SM89), 8,192 paths and one European-call row took
0.61 ms at 252 dates, **1.18 ms at 504 dates** (`dt = 1/504`),
2.58 ms at 1,008 dates, and 5.78 ms at 2,016 dates
(median of five CUDA-event runs after five warmups, including row, kernel,
random-number, path and payoff work, excluding allocation and host/device
transfers). A four-row batch took 3.54 ms for 16 rows at 252 dates,
**7.68 ms at 504 dates**, and 41.51 ms at 2,016 dates. For 32,768 paths per row, one row took 0.94 ms at
252 dates and 4.79 ms at 1,008 dates. Results are specific to this GPU and
launch profile. Reproduce one case with
`build/ai_factory_causal_fft_benchmark 504 8192 1 1` after building that
CMake target.

At the dataset grid (252 days, 504 simulation dates, 8,192 paths per
row), the measured GPU medians provide this cost comparison:

| Method | 1 row | 16 rows |
|---|---:|---:|
| Gaussian rough-Bergomi one-shot FFT | 0.40 ms | 6.21 ms |
| Causal rough-Heston FFT | 1.18 ms | 7.68 ms |
| Rough-Heston seven-factor lift | 13.92 ms | 13.92 ms |
| Rough-Heston direct convolution prototype | 6.00 ms | 42.48 ms |

The first row prices a different model. The lift uses a different time
scheme. The direct prototype in `/tmp/causal_fft_multiline_batch.cu` includes
RNG generation in the timing but prepares its weights outside it; the causal
benchmark includes GPU weight and spectrum preparation. This table describes
cost and cannot establish price equivalence.

Existing rough price and sample datasets use a one-day convention of
`1/252` year and **two simulation steps per day**, so their actual simulation
step is `1/504` year. The benchmark holds maturity at 252 days while varying
the number of simulation dates; only its 504-date case uses that dataset
step. This causal engine is not yet a catalogue dataset binding.

For the repository's [validation protocol](../validation/query.md),
the causal Euler/hybrid cell gives `0.083652 ± 0.000290` at `dt = 1/504`
with 131,072 paths (7.87 ms on the measured GPU), while the continuous-model
Fourier price is `0.07905466`. The exact Fourier price of the fitted seven-factor
lift fitted for `dt = 1/360` is `0.07911481`, and its existing million-path
CUDA fixture at that step gives `0.07916836 ± 0.00009591`. The lift's kernel approximation error is therefore
about `0.00006016` for this parameter set, while the causal cell's error at
the dataset step is about `0.004598`. The lift price is not itself the
continuous-model oracle.

With the same model, seed, and 131,072 paths, the causal engine gives
`0.08162564 ± 0.00028074` at 2,016 steps and
`0.08092010 ± 0.00027816` at 8,192 steps. Refining the grid reduces the gap
but leaves it statistically visible. The FFT decomposition agrees with direct
convolution of the *same* discrete cell; an FP64 direct check changes its price
by less than `0.000002` on 8,192 paths. Diagnostic controls with zero
volatility of variance or `H ≈ 1/2` agree with their Fourier references within
Monte Carlo error. At `H = 0.1`, `rho = 0`, and positive volatility of
variance, the gap remains. A direct 504-step sample finds negative raw
variance on about 28% of sampled path dates; the policy uses `max(V, 0)` in
the diffusion and spot updates. These observations locate the problem in the
rough stochastic-variance time discretization, rather than the FFT update or
spot correlation alone. They do not isolate a single term of the weak error.
The causal cell still needs a separately validated time scheme before it can
replace an established pricing binding. No sensitivity calculation is exposed
by this engine yet.
